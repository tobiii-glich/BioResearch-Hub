Ecology Study Notes 🔬

Learn how living organisms interact with one another and with their physical environment.

1. Introduction to Ecology

Ecology is the branch of biology that studies the relationships between organisms and their environment.

It examines how organisms interact with one another and with non-living factors such as water, temperature, light, soil, and air.

2. Levels of Ecological Organization

  • Organism — an individual living thing.
  • Population — organisms of the same species living in an area.
  • Community — different populations living together.
  • Ecosystem — a community and its physical environment.
  • Biosphere — the global zone where life exists.

3. Ecosystem

An ecosystem consists of living organisms and the non-living components of their environment interacting as a system.

Components of an ecosystem

  • Biotic factors — living components.
  • Abiotic factors — non-living components.

4. Food Chain

A food chain shows the transfer of food energy from one organism to another.

A simple example is:

Grass → Grasshopper → Frog → Snake → Hawk

Producers form the beginning of most food chains because they manufacture food using energy from the sun.

5. Food Web

A food web is a network of interconnected food chains within an ecosystem.

Food webs provide a more realistic representation of feeding relationships because organisms may feed on several different organisms.

6. Energy Flow in an Ecosystem

Energy enters most ecosystems through sunlight. Producers capture this energy through photosynthesis.

Energy is then transferred through consumers and decomposers. Some energy is lost as heat at each stage of transfer.

7. Ecological Relationships

  • Predation — one organism feeds on another.
  • Competition — organisms compete for limited resources.
  • Mutualism — both organisms benefit.
  • Commensalism — one benefits while the other is not significantly affected.
  • Parasitism — one organism benefits while the host is harmed.

8. Population

A population is a group of organisms of the same species living in a particular area at a particular time.

Factors affecting population size

  • Birth rate
  • Death rate
  • Immigration
  • Emigration
  • Availability of food and water
  • Predation
  • Disease

9. Biodiversity

Biodiversity refers to the variety of living organisms found in an area or on Earth.

Biodiversity includes the variety of species, genetic differences, and ecosystems.

Importance of biodiversity

  • Maintains ecosystem stability.
  • Provides food and other resources.
  • Supports scientific research.
  • Provides habitats for organisms.

10. Conservation

Conservation is the protection and careful management of natural resources and living organisms.

Ways to conserve biodiversity

  • Protect natural habitats.
  • Prevent illegal hunting.
  • Plant and protect trees.
  • Reduce pollution.
  • Use natural resources responsibly.
