🔬 Cell Biology: Detailed Study Notes

1. What is a Cell?

A cell is the basic structural and functional unit of life. All living organisms are made up of one or more cells.

Some organisms, such as bacteria, consist of a single cell, while organisms such as humans, plants, and animals are made up of many cells.

2. Cell Theory

The modern cell theory states that:

  • All living organisms are made up of one or more cells.
  • The cell is the basic unit of structure and function in living organisms.
  • New cells arise from pre-existing cells.
  • Cells contain hereditary information that can be passed to daughter cells.

3. Types of Cells

🦠 Prokaryotic Cells

Prokaryotic cells are generally small and simple cells that do not have a membrane-bound nucleus.

Bacteria are examples of organisms with prokaryotic cells.

🧬 Eukaryotic Cells

Eukaryotic cells have a true nucleus surrounded by a nuclear membrane and contain membrane-bound organelles.

Plants, animals, fungi, and many protists have eukaryotic cells.

4. Cell Organelles and Their Functions

  • Nucleus: Contains genetic material and controls many cell activities.
  • Cell membrane: Controls movement of substances into and out of the cell.
  • Cytoplasm: Where many chemical reactions occur.
  • Mitochondria: Site of aerobic respiration and major ATP production.
  • Ribosomes: Site of protein synthesis.
  • Endoplasmic reticulum: Helps produce and transport proteins and other substances.
  • Golgi apparatus: Modifies, sorts, and packages certain cell products.
  • Lysosomes: Contain digestive enzymes that break down materials.
  • Chloroplasts: Site of photosynthesis in plant cells.
  • Vacuole: Stores substances; plant cell vacuoles also help maintain cell pressure.

5. Plant Cells and Animal Cells

Plant and animal cells are both eukaryotic, but they have important structural differences.

  • Plant cells have a cellulose cell wall.
  • Plant cells contain chloroplasts.
  • Plant cells usually have a large permanent vacuole.
  • Animal cells do not have a cell wall.
  • Animal cells do not normally contain chloroplasts.

6. Cell Membrane

The cell membrane is a selectively permeable boundary around the cell. It controls the movement of substances between the cell and its surroundings.

It is mainly made up of phospholipids and proteins and is commonly described using the fluid mosaic model.

7. Diffusion

Diffusion is the net movement of particles from a region of higher concentration to a region of lower concentration, down a concentration gradient.

Diffusion does not require cellular energy in the form of ATP.

Example: Oxygen can diffuse from the air in the alveoli into the blood.

8. Osmosis

Osmosis is the net movement of water molecules through a selectively permeable membrane from a region of higher water potential to a region of lower water potential.

Osmosis is important in maintaining water balance in cells.

9. Active Transport

Active transport is the movement of substances across a cell membrane against their concentration gradient using cellular energy.

It commonly requires carrier proteins and energy from ATP.

Example: Mineral ions can be actively absorbed by plant roots.

10. Cell Division

Cell division is the process by which a cell produces new cells. It is important for growth, repair, reproduction, and replacement of damaged cells.

Mitosis

Mitosis produces genetically similar daughter cells and is important for growth and tissue repair.

Meiosis

Meiosis produces cells with half the usual chromosome number and contributes to genetic variation during sexual reproduction.

11. Stages of Mitosis

  1. Prophase: Chromosomes condense and become visible.
  2. Metaphase: Chromosomes line up near the middle of the cell.
  3. Anaphase: Sister chromatids separate.
  4. Telophase: New nuclear membranes form around the chromosome sets.
  5. Cytokinesis: The cytoplasm divides to form two daughter cells.

12. Cell Specialization

Cell specialization is the process by which cells develop structures and functions suited to particular roles.

Examples

  • Red blood cells: Specialized for transporting oxygen.
  • Root hair cells: Specialized for absorbing water and mineral ions.
  • Neurones: Specialized for transmitting electrical signals.
  • Sperm cells: Specialized for reaching and fertilizing an egg cell.
  • Palisade cells: Specialized for photosynthesis.

13. Microscopy

Microscopes are instruments used to observe structures that cannot be seen clearly with the unaided eye.

Light microscopes use visible light and lenses, while electron microscopes use beams of electrons and can reveal much finer structural details.

Magnification:

Magnification = Image size ÷ Actual size

14. Importance of Cells

  • They form the structures of living organisms.
  • They carry out essential life processes.
  • They produce and use energy.
  • They contain genetic information.
  • They allow organisms to grow and repair damaged tissues.
  • They enable reproduction and development.

15. Quick Cell Biology Revision

  • Cell: Basic structural and functional unit of life.
  • Nucleus: Contains genetic material.
  • Mitochondria: Major site of aerobic respiration.
  • Ribosomes: Protein synthesis.
  • Chloroplast: Photosynthesis.
  • Diffusion: Movement down a concentration gradient.
  • Osmosis: Movement of water through a selectively permeable membrane.
  • Active transport: Movement against a concentration gradient using energy.
  • Mitosis: Produces genetically similar daughter cells.
  • Meiosis: Produces haploid cells for sexual reproduction.
