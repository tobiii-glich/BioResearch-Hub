1. What Are Enzymes?

Enzymes are biological catalysts produced by living organisms. They speed up chemical reactions in cells without being permanently used up in the reactions.

Key idea: Enzymes increase the rate of biochemical reactions by lowering the activation energy required for the reaction.

2. Properties of Enzymes

  • Most enzymes are proteins.
  • They are biological catalysts.
  • They are highly specific in their action.
  • They are not permanently used up during reactions.
  • They work best under particular temperature and pH conditions.
  • Small amounts of enzymes can catalyse many reactions.

3. Enzyme and Substrate

The substance on which an enzyme acts is called the substrate.

The part of an enzyme where the substrate binds is called the active site.

Enzyme + Substrate → Enzyme-Substrate Complex → Enzyme + Product

4. Lock-and-Key Model

The lock-and-key model proposes that the active site of an enzyme has a specific shape that fits its substrate.

This helps explain why enzymes are generally specific to particular substrates.

5. Induced-Fit Model

The induced-fit model explains that the active site can undergo a slight change in shape when the substrate binds.

This allows the enzyme and substrate to fit together more effectively during the reaction.

6. Effect of Temperature

Increasing temperature generally increases enzyme activity up to an optimum temperature.

Above the optimum temperature, the bonds maintaining the enzyme's structure can be disrupted. The enzyme may become denatured and lose its normal activity.

7. Effect of pH

Every enzyme has a pH range in which it works most effectively. Changes in pH can affect the shape and charge of the active site.

Extreme pH conditions can cause an enzyme to lose its normal structure and activity.

8. Effect of Substrate Concentration

When substrate concentration increases, the rate of an enzyme-controlled reaction usually increases until the available active sites become occupied.

At this point, adding more substrate has little or no further effect on the reaction rate if enzyme concentration remains unchanged.

9. Enzyme Inhibitors

Enzyme inhibitors are substances that reduce or prevent enzyme activity.

  • Competitive inhibitors: compete with the substrate for the active site.
  • Non-competitive inhibitors: bind to another part of an enzyme and can alter the shape of its active site.

10. Examples of Enzymes

  • Amylase: breaks down starch.
  • Protease: breaks down proteins.
  • Lipase: breaks down fats.
  • Catalase: breaks down hydrogen peroxide.

11. Importance of Enzymes

Enzymes are essential because many chemical reactions required for life would occur too slowly without them.

  • Digestion of food
  • Cellular respiration
  • DNA replication
  • Photosynthesis
  • Metabolism
  • Production of biological substances
