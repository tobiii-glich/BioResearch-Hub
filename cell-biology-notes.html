Cell Biology Study Notes 🧫

Learn about the structure and functions of cells, cellular transport, cell division, and cellular processes.

1. Introduction to Cell Biology

Cell biology is the branch of biology concerned with the study of cells, their structures, functions, and activities.

The cell is the basic structural and functional unit of living organisms.

2. Cell Theory

Cell theory states that:

  • All living organisms are made up of one or more cells.
  • The cell is the basic unit of life.
  • All cells come from pre-existing cells.

3. Types of Cells

Prokaryotic Cells

Prokaryotic cells do not have a membrane-bound nucleus. Bacteria are examples of prokaryotic organisms.

Eukaryotic Cells

Eukaryotic cells contain a membrane-bound nucleus and other membrane-bound organelles.

Plants, animals, fungi, and many protists have eukaryotic cells.

4. Cell Organelles

  • Nucleus — contains genetic material and controls cell activities.
  • Mitochondria — site of most aerobic respiration and ATP production.
  • Ribosomes — involved in protein synthesis.
  • Endoplasmic reticulum — involved in the production and transport of substances.
  • Golgi apparatus — modifies, sorts, and packages substances.
  • Lysosomes — contain enzymes that help digest cellular materials.
  • Chloroplasts — carry out photosynthesis in plant cells.
  • Vacuoles — store water, dissolved substances, and other materials.

5. Plant and Animal Cells

Plant and animal cells are both eukaryotic, but they have some important differences.

  • Plant cells have a cell wall; animal cells do not.
  • Plant cells usually contain chloroplasts; animal cells do not.
  • Plant cells usually have a large central vacuole.
  • Animal cells generally have smaller vacuoles.

6. Cell Membrane

The cell membrane surrounds the cell and controls the movement of substances into and out of the cell.

It is selectively permeable, meaning that it allows certain substances to pass through more easily than others.

7. Movement of Substances

Diffusion

Diffusion is the net movement of particles from a region of higher concentration to a region of lower concentration.

Osmosis

Osmosis is the movement of water molecules through a selectively permeable membrane from a region of higher water potential to a region of lower water potential.

Active Transport

Active transport is the movement of substances against their concentration gradient using energy.

8. Cell Division

Mitosis

Mitosis produces two genetically similar daughter cells. It is important for growth, tissue repair, and asexual reproduction in some organisms.

Meiosis

Meiosis produces cells with half the usual chromosome number. It is involved in the formation of gametes in organisms that reproduce sexually.

9. Cellular Respiration

Cellular respiration is the process by which cells release energy from food molecules.

In aerobic respiration, glucose is broken down in the presence of oxygen to release energy.

The mitochondria are an important site of aerobic respiration in eukaryotic cells.

10. Cell Adaptations

Cells may have specialised structures that help them perform particular functions efficiently.

  • Red blood cells — specialised for transporting oxygen.
  • Root hair cells — adapted for absorbing water and mineral ions.
  • Nerve cells — specialised for transmitting electrical signals.
  • Sperm cells — adapted for movement and delivery of genetic material.
