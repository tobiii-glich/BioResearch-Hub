DNA & RNA

A complete study note on DNA, RNA, genetic information, replication, transcription, translation, and mutations.

1. What Is DNA?

DNA stands for Deoxyribonucleic Acid. It is the main genetic material in most living organisms.

DNA stores the instructions needed for the growth, development, functioning, and reproduction of organisms.

2. Structure of DNA

DNA consists of two long strands twisted around each other to form a structure called a double helix.

Each DNA nucleotide contains three parts:

  • A deoxyribose sugar
  • A phosphate group
  • A nitrogenous base

DNA Bases

  • Adenine — A
  • Thymine — T
  • Cytosine — C
  • Guanine — G
A pairs with T   |   C pairs with G

3. What Is RNA?

RNA stands for Ribonucleic Acid.

RNA plays important roles in the expression of genetic information and protein production.

4. Structure of RNA

RNA is generally made up of a single strand of nucleotides.

Each RNA nucleotide contains:

  • Ribose sugar
  • Phosphate group
  • Nitrogenous base

RNA Bases

  • Adenine — A
  • Uracil — U
  • Cytosine — C
  • Guanine — G

In RNA, uracil replaces thymine.

5. Types of RNA

Messenger RNA (mRNA)

mRNA carries genetic information from DNA to the ribosome for protein production.

Transfer RNA (tRNA)

tRNA carries specific amino acids to the ribosome during protein synthesis.

Ribosomal RNA (rRNA)

rRNA forms an important part of ribosomes, where proteins are assembled.

6. DNA Replication

DNA replication is the process by which DNA makes a copy of itself before cell division.

  1. The DNA double helix unwinds.
  2. The hydrogen bonds between complementary bases break.
  3. The two DNA strands separate.
  4. New complementary nucleotides are added.
  5. Two DNA molecules are produced.
DNA replication is described as semi-conservative because each new DNA molecule contains one original strand and one newly synthesized strand.

7. Transcription

Transcription is the process of making an RNA molecule using DNA as a template.

  1. The DNA region containing the required gene opens.
  2. An RNA strand is formed using the DNA template.
  3. RNA nucleotides pair with complementary DNA bases.
  4. The RNA molecule separates from the DNA.
DNA → RNA

8. Translation

Translation is the process by which the information carried by mRNA is used to assemble a protein.

  1. mRNA attaches to a ribosome.
  2. The ribosome reads the mRNA codons.
  3. tRNA brings the appropriate amino acids.
  4. Amino acids are joined together.
  5. A protein is produced.
DNA → RNA → Protein

9. Genetic Code

The genetic code is the set of rules by which information in DNA and RNA determines the amino acid sequence of proteins.

A group of three bases on mRNA is called a codon.

10. DNA and RNA Differences

Feature DNA RNA
Sugar Deoxyribose Ribose
Structure Usually double-stranded Usually single-stranded
Base Thymine Uracil
Main role Stores genetic information Helps express genetic information

11. Mutations

A mutation is a change in the DNA sequence.

Mutations can occur naturally or can result from environmental factors that damage DNA.

Some mutations have little or no noticeable effect, while others can affect an organism's characteristics or biological functions.

12. Importance of DNA and RNA

  • DNA stores hereditary information.
  • DNA allows genetic information to be passed to offspring.
  • RNA helps in protein synthesis.
  • Genes influence the characteristics of organisms.
  • DNA and RNA are important in modern biotechnology and medicine.
