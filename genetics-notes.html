🧬 Genetics Study Notes

BioResearch Hub

1. Meaning of Genetics

Genetics is the branch of biology that deals with heredity and variation in living organisms.

2. Heredity

Heredity is the transmission of characteristics from parents to their offspring through genetic information.

3. Variation

Variation refers to differences in characteristics among individuals of the same species.

Continuous variation: Characteristics show a range of values, such as height.

Discontinuous variation: Characteristics fall into distinct categories, such as ABO blood groups.

4. Gene

A gene is a section of DNA involved in determining a particular characteristic or producing a functional product.

5. Chromosomes

Chromosomes are structures made mainly of DNA and proteins. They carry genes and organize genetic information.

6. DNA

DNA stands for deoxyribonucleic acid. It stores genetic information and can be copied so genetic information can be passed to new cells.

7. Alleles

Alleles are different forms of the same gene.

8. Dominant and Recessive Alleles

A dominant allele can contribute to the phenotype when present in a heterozygous individual.

A recessive allele generally contributes to the phenotype when two copies are present in a simple Mendelian pattern.

9. Genotype and Phenotype

Genotype: The genetic combination carried by an organism.

Phenotype: The observable characteristics of an organism.

10. Homozygous and Heterozygous

Homozygous: Two identical alleles.

Heterozygous: Two different alleles.

Homozygous → AA or aa
Heterozygous → Aa

11. Mendel and Genetics

Gregor Mendel carried out important experiments on pea plants that helped establish principles of inheritance.

12. Mendel's Laws

Law of Segregation: The two alleles of a gene separate during gamete formation.

Independent Assortment: Alleles of different unlinked genes can assort independently during gamete formation.

13. Monohybrid Cross

A monohybrid cross follows the inheritance of one pair of contrasting characteristics.

Aa × Aa

Possible offspring: AA, Aa, Aa, aa

Genotype ratio: 1 : 2 : 1

14. Dihybrid Cross

A dihybrid cross follows the inheritance of two different characteristics at the same time.

Classic F₂ phenotypic ratio:
9 : 3 : 3 : 1

15. Sex Determination

In humans, females typically have XX sex chromosomes while males typically have XY sex chromosomes.

16. Mutation

A mutation is a change in genetic material. Mutations may be harmful, beneficial, or have little noticeable effect.

17. Genetic Disorders

Examples include:

  • Sickle cell disease
  • Haemophilia
  • Down syndrome
  • Cystic fibrosis

18. Genetic Engineering

Genetic engineering involves deliberately modifying genetic material using biotechnology.

19. Importance of Genetics

  • Understanding inheritance.
  • Studying genetic diseases.
  • Improving crops and livestock.
  • Understanding evolution and variation.
  • Supporting medical research.

20. Quick Revision

  • Genetics: Study of heredity and variation.
  • Gene: Section of DNA.
  • Allele: Alternative form of a gene.
  • Genotype: Genetic constitution.
  • Phenotype: Observable characteristics.
  • Mutation: Change in genetic material.
