Microbiology Study Notes 🔬

Learn about microorganisms, their structures, activities, importance, and effects on living organisms.

1. Introduction to Microbiology

Microbiology is the branch of biology concerned with the study of microorganisms. These organisms are generally too small to be seen clearly with the unaided eye.

Microbiology involves the study of microorganisms such as bacteria, viruses, fungi, protozoa, and microscopic algae.

2. Types of Microorganisms

  • Bacteria
  • Viruses
  • Fungi
  • Protozoa
  • Microscopic algae

Microorganisms may be useful, harmless, or harmful depending on the organism and the environment in which it occurs.

3. Bacteria

Bacteria are microscopic single-celled organisms. They are prokaryotic because their genetic material is not enclosed within a membrane-bound nucleus.

Common bacterial shapes

  • Coccus — spherical or round-shaped.
  • Bacillus — rod-shaped.
  • Spirillum — spiral-shaped.
  • Vibrio — comma-shaped.

4. Viruses

Viruses are extremely small infectious agents that contain genetic material surrounded by a protein coat.

Viruses cannot reproduce independently. They require a suitable living host cell to make new virus particles.

5. Fungi

Fungi are eukaryotic organisms. They include organisms such as yeasts and moulds.

Many fungi obtain nutrients by secreting enzymes onto organic material and absorbing the resulting soluble substances.

6. Protozoa

Protozoa are mostly microscopic, single-celled eukaryotic organisms. Many live in water or moist environments.

Some protozoa are free-living, while others live as parasites in other organisms.

7. Useful Microorganisms

  • Used in food production.
  • Help in decomposition of organic matter.
  • Used in the production of some medicines.
  • Help recycle nutrients in ecosystems.
  • Used in biotechnology and scientific research.

8. Harmful Microorganisms

Some microorganisms can cause diseases in humans, animals, and plants. Others can spoil food or damage materials.

Disease-causing microorganisms are called pathogens.

9. Microbial Nutrition

Microorganisms obtain nutrients in different ways. Some produce their own food, while others obtain organic substances from their surroundings.

  • Autotrophic nutrition
  • Heterotrophic nutrition
  • Saprophytic nutrition
  • Parasitic nutrition

10. Microbial Reproduction

Many bacteria reproduce through binary fission. In this process, one bacterial cell divides to form two daughter cells.

Some fungi reproduce using spores, while microorganisms have other reproductive processes depending on their group.
